# CNC Testhook load calibration

Calibration → Load creates a one-parameter sweep from the current printer, process, and first filament. The supplied `testHook_V2 (1).stl` is bundled, unchanged, as **CNC Testhook** in the convenient-model menu.

1. Select printer/filament/process settings in Prepare. Configure support and brim for upright hooks as needed.
2. In Calibration → Load, enter material name, material type, and color. Choose the parameter and comma-separated values, or use start/end/increment to build a numeric range. Enum options list their valid serialized values beneath the form.
3. Choose repeats per value **per orientation**. Three repeats at three values produce 18 hooks: nine XY and nine Z.
4. Generate and slice the study. XY preserves the supplied flat geometry (35 × 70 × 9 mm); Z rotates it 90° about X, putting the 70 mm axis upright. Every hook gets its own plate and stable ID, for independent cooling and unambiguous results. Each plate is exported to a native `.3mf` and `.gcode` in the study folder. Completed exports get versioned geometry completion records; older outputs are regenerated by Resume after the bed-placement fix; Cancel and Resume preserve completed work.
5. Use Open study folder to access the outputs, or select multiple result rows and **Open selected hooks in Prepare** to inspect them together. Compatible hooks are arranged with clearance, overflowing onto additional plates. Both orientations contact the bed at Z=0. Physical hook IDs should match the file/plate labels; the specimen geometry is not embossed or altered.
6. Record every specimen as Failed, Did not break (maximum tested load), or Not tested. Notes distinguish print failures, lost parts, and omitted tests. Load units can be N, kgf, or lbf and convert when changed. Optional mass is the actual hook mass without support/brim. Valid table edits save automatically; Save results and calculate also persists metadata.
7. Reopen saved studies to continue results later. Select a reference value, a base mechanical material, a calculation mode (Regressed by default), and either a new or existing calibrated material when publishing to the Load material selector. All study observations are saved, including untested and surviving hooks.

## What the calculations mean

Results remain separated by parameter value and orientation. Only actual tensile failures contribute to the mean, minimum, sample standard deviation, and mean failure-force/mass ratio. Survived samples are censored lower bounds, and untested specimens are excluded. The table retains both.

Failure force in newtons is a property of the specimen, fixture, and print settings; it is not itself a material stress. Publishing an effective tensile calibration requires validated **peak tensile stress per applied force (MPa/N)** for the actual hook and fixture in XY and Z. These factors can come from a separately validated unit-load model. Do not substitute an arbitrary cross-sectional area: this hook carries nonuniform stress. The factors and selected results are retained with the calibration.

Regressed mode uses failure measurements across accumulated studies. Numeric parameters need at least three distinct settings to fit a trend; replicate configurations receive equal weight. Constrained ridge main effects are selected using cross-validation that holds out entire configurations, with shrinkage and a pooled-mean fallback when no trend improves validation error. Infill density and wall/shell counts have nonnegative slopes; thermal settings may use one concave optimum with at least five configurations. Categorical settings use matching measured categories, never numeric interpolation. Thermal extrapolation holds at measured limits, density is bounded to 0–100%, and extrapolation is identified in the Materials page. These are effective property estimates, not certified material allowables.

Simple mode uses the minimum at matching tested settings. Both modes need at least two usable measurements for a property; insufficient evidence retains the base estimate. Survived hooks remain lower bounds, not failure values, and do not enter either fit. Excluded and untested observations are retained but ignored. Re-saving the same study updates stable hook IDs instead of adding duplicates; newly saved studies add evidence. Exclusions survive re-saving a study.

XY/Z stress-per-force conversions determine effective ultimate tensile limits. Inherited yield estimates are capped at the corresponding ultimate limit and labeled accordingly. Elastic moduli, shear properties, Poisson ratio, and density retain the base material's values until separate measurements are entered. Weight is used for N/g comparisons and does not infer density or elastic properties.

## Materials page

Calibration → Materials lists saved calibrations and supports importing/exporting versioned JSON libraries, editing name/type/color, per-parameter modes, reference settings, and expected property ranges. The data sheet distinguishes calculated and inherited values, shows the usable point count and base comparison, and shows position within a user-supplied expected range. Generic material presets do not provide validated manufacturer ranges, so missing ranges are explicitly shown as not supplied.

Select a property and parameter to inspect its curve and optionally overlay experimental points. Change the query value to inspect interpolation/extrapolation. The observations table retains identities, settings, outcomes, source studies, and notes. **Exclude / restore selected data** removes faulty observations from fitting without losing the original record. **Add measured property** records separate measurements in the units shown; this allows other mechanical properties to replace defaults as evidence becomes available. Numeric graphs show raw points for the property; when multiple parameters vary, the curve holds the other reference settings fixed.

Static and transient simulations evaluate experimental properties using current print settings. Print-setting and mass/strength recommendations replace supported tensile parameter factors with empirical directional ratios, retaining estimates for unmeasured effects. Geometry, stiffness, print-time, and other assumptions are not inferred from tensile failure loads alone. Recommendations remain estimates and should be validated by a new simulation.

Library edits preserve a `materials.previous.json` recovery copy, which can be imported through the Materials page. Imports create new material identities, avoiding accidental overwrites. Older simple calibrations remain usable; saving new observations to one recovers its source study's observations when that study is still available locally.

Calibrated material names appear as `(Material) -- CALIBRATED`. They retain the material type, color, source study, parameter, and value in provenance. Updating an existing entry preserves its key. Previously saved load-analysis projects retain their embedded material snapshot until the user explicitly selects the updated library entry.

## Scope and persistence

Supported sweeps: infill pattern/density, wall loops, top/bottom surface patterns, layer height, line width, minimum layer time, nozzle and bed temperatures, plate type, top/bottom shell counts, outer/inner wall and infill speeds, maximum fan speed, flow ratio, and infill direction. Nozzle/bed temperature sweeps update initial-layer temperatures too. Minimum layer time is a cooling target; actual layers may take longer.

Studies allow 1–50 unique setting values, 1–20 repeats per orientation, and at most 200 total hooks. Each output file is a separate plate, so the in-memory 36-plate limit does not constrain the study. Batch slicing uses one hook per output plate for independent thermal history. Opening a selection packs compatible object settings together, separates incompatible global settings, and keeps minimum-layer-time specimens on individual plates. Opening a selection respects the slicer’s 36-plate limit; larger selections can be opened in subsets.

Data is local in `<slicer data directory>/load_calibration/`: versioned study JSON, an exact copy of the hook STL, per-hook project/G-code/completion files, and a versioned `materials.json` library. Generation runs on a cancellable background worker and leaves the current Prepare project unchanged. Failed exports do not receive completion records and are retried by Resume.

## Verification

`[LoadCalibration]` in `libslic3r_tests` covers range bounds, identity, censored observations, statistics, invalid inputs, mechanical-property preservation, native setting application, geometry orientation, printer-bed fit, and native `.3mf` round trips. The matching `fff_print_tests` test slices the real bundled STL in both orientations and checks settings and extrusion output.

`[MaterialExperiments]` covers interpolation/extrapolation, censored and excluded data, accumulated/upserted studies, simple/categorical behavior, trend rejection, serialization, inherited properties, and empirical recommendation factors. Multi-plate tests check bed contact, spacing, orientation and object settings after native 3MF reload. Slicing tests exercise generic and Bambu profiles in both orientations.
